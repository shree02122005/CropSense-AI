# Soil texture and soil pH

## Soil texture classes
Texture is the proportion of sand, silt and clay. The USDA texture triangle gives 12 classes: sand, loamy sand, sandy loam, loam, silt loam, silt, sandy clay loam, clay loam, silty clay loam, sandy clay, silty clay and clay.

## What texture means for farming
- Sandy soils (sand, loamy sand): drain quickly, warm up fast and are easy to work, but hold little water and nutrients. They need frequent, light irrigation, organic matter and split fertiliser doses. Suitable for groundnut, millets such as pearl millet, watermelon, many vegetables and root crops with good management.
- Loamy soils (sandy loam, loam, silt loam): the best general-purpose agricultural soils with a balance of drainage, water holding and fertility. Suitable for most crops including vegetables, wheat, maize, pulses and oilseeds.
- Clay loams and clays: hold much water and nutrients and have high CEC, but drain slowly, can waterlog, become sticky when wet and hard and cracked when dry. Suitable for rice (which tolerates standing water), cotton, sugarcane, chickpea and sorghum on deep clay soils. Need good drainage for crops that do not tolerate waterlogging.
- Silty soils: fertile and hold water well but form crusts easily after rain, which can hurt seedling emergence; organic matter and mulching help.

## Soil pH classes (USDA)
Extremely acidic: below 4.5. Very strongly acidic: 4.5 to 5.0. Strongly acidic: 5.1 to 5.5. Moderately acidic: 5.6 to 6.0. Slightly acidic: 6.1 to 6.5. Neutral: 6.6 to 7.3. Slightly alkaline: 7.4 to 7.8. Moderately alkaline: 7.9 to 8.4. Strongly alkaline: 8.5 to 9.0. Very strongly alkaline: above 9.0.

## Why pH matters
- Most crops grow best between pH 6.0 and 7.5, where most nutrients are most available.
- In strongly acidic soils (below about 5.5), phosphorus becomes fixed by iron and aluminium, aluminium and manganese can become toxic, and calcium, magnesium and molybdenum may be deficient. Liming (agricultural lime or dolomite, based on a lime-requirement test) raises pH.
- In alkaline soils (above about 7.8), phosphorus is fixed by calcium, and zinc, iron, manganese and boron become less available. Above about 8.5 the soil may be sodic (high exchangeable sodium), causing poor structure and water infiltration; gypsum application based on a gypsum-requirement test is the usual remedy, along with organic matter and good drainage.
- Acid-tolerant crops include tea, coffee, potato, pineapple and finger millet (ragi), which tolerates a wide range. Crops that prefer near-neutral to slightly alkaline soils include chickpea, cotton, sorghum, wheat and mustard. Barley and some sorghum varieties tolerate moderately saline-alkaline conditions better than most crops.

## Measuring pH
Soil pH changes slowly but varies within a farm. A lab or Soil Health Card test of the actual field is more reliable than map estimates. Soil pH is usually measured in a 1:2.5 soil:water suspension.
