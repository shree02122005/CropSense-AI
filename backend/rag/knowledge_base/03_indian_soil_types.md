# Major soil types of India and Karnataka

## Black soils (regur / black cotton soils)
Found on the Deccan plateau, including north Karnataka (for example Belagavi, Dharwad, Vijayapura, Kalaburagi, Raichur), Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat. High clay content (often 40 to 60%) dominated by swelling clays, high CEC, high water-holding capacity, neutral to alkaline pH (about 7.5 to 8.5), usually low in nitrogen and organic carbon and variable in phosphorus. They swell and become sticky when wet and shrink and crack deeply when dry. Well suited to cotton, sorghum (jowar), chickpea, wheat, sunflower, safflower, pigeonpea, soybean and sugarcane under irrigation. Drainage is the main concern in heavy rains; deep cracks allow rapid water entry in the first rains.

## Red soils
The most widespread soils in south Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Formed from granite and gneiss; red colour from iron oxides. Usually sandy loam to sandy clay loam, porous and well drained, low to moderate CEC, slightly acidic to neutral pH, low in nitrogen, phosphorus, organic matter and often zinc. Prone to crusting and erosion. Suited to finger millet (ragi), groundnut, pulses such as pigeonpea, horse gram and field bean, maize, millets, tobacco and, with irrigation, vegetables, mulberry, coconut and fruit crops.

## Laterite and lateritic soils
Found in high-rainfall areas such as the Western Ghats and coastal Karnataka, Kerala, Goa and parts of Odisha. Strongly leached, acidic (pH often 4.5 to 6.0), low CEC, low in bases, phosphorus and often boron, rich in iron and aluminium oxides, sometimes gravelly. Suited to plantation crops such as coffee, tea, rubber, cashew, arecanut, coconut, pepper and cardamom, and to rice in valleys. Liming and organic matter improve them for field crops.

## Alluvial soils
Found in the Indo-Gangetic plains, river valleys and deltas, including parts of coastal Karnataka and river valleys. Texture ranges from sandy loam to clay; usually fertile, with near-neutral to alkaline pH and good potassium, though often low in nitrogen and organic carbon. Suited to rice, wheat, sugarcane, maize, pulses, oilseeds, jute and vegetables.

## Arid and desert (sandy) soils
Found in Rajasthan and dry parts of Gujarat and Haryana, and sandy patches elsewhere. Sandy, low organic matter, low water-holding capacity, often alkaline and sometimes saline. Suited to pearl millet (bajra), moth bean, cluster bean (guar), mustard and, with irrigation, groundnut and cumin.

## Saline and sodic soils
Occur in irrigated command areas with poor drainage and in arid regions. Saline soils have high soluble salts (high EC); sodic soils have high exchangeable sodium and pH above about 8.5. They need reclamation (gypsum for sodic soils, leaching with good-quality water and drainage for saline soils) and salt-tolerant crops such as barley, cotton, sugar beet, some rice and wheat varieties.

## Karnataka agro-climatic note
Karnataka has 10 agro-climatic zones. The north has mostly black soils with low rainfall (rabi sorghum, chickpea, sunflower, cotton); the south interior has mostly red soils (ragi, groundnut, pulses, mulberry); the hill zone and coast have laterite and red soils with high rainfall (coffee, arecanut, pepper, rice, coconut).
