# Cropping seasons in India and Karnataka

## Kharif (monsoon season)
Sown with the onset of the south-west monsoon, roughly June to July, and harvested about September to November. Typical kharif crops: rice, maize, sorghum, pearl millet, finger millet (ragi), pigeonpea, green gram, black gram, groundnut, soybean, cotton, sunflower and many vegetables. Timely sowing after enough rain (for example after about 50 to 75 mm of cumulative rain wets the soil) is important in rainfed areas.

## Rabi (post-monsoon / winter season)
Sown roughly October to December and harvested about February to April. Typical rabi crops: wheat, chickpea, rabi sorghum, safflower, mustard, lentil, linseed, rabi onion, potato in the plains and many vegetables. In north Karnataka black soils, rabi sorghum, chickpea and sunflower grow on soil moisture stored from the monsoon.

## Zaid (summer season)
Short season between rabi and kharif, roughly March to June, under irrigation. Typical crops: green gram, black gram, watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber and other vegetables, fodder crops and summer groundnut or sunflower.

## Karnataka notes
Karnataka receives both the south-west monsoon (June to September) and, in the south and east, part of the north-east monsoon (October to December). The coastal and Malnad regions receive very high rainfall (often 2500 mm or more) and are suited to rice, arecanut, coconut, coffee, pepper and cardamom. The north interior is semi-arid with black soils; the south interior plateau has red soils with moderate rainfall and grows ragi, maize, groundnut, pulses, mulberry and horticultural crops.

## Matching crops to weather
Crop choice should consider expected rainfall and irrigation availability for the whole season, not just today's weather. Current temperature and humidity from a weather service describe only the present day; seasonal forecasts from the India Meteorological Department (IMD) and agro-advisories from Krishi Vigyan Kendras are more useful for planning.
