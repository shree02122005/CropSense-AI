# Irrigation guidelines

## Soil texture and water
- Sandy soils hold little water (often below about 100 mm per metre of soil), so they need frequent, light irrigations; drip and sprinkler irrigation work well.
- Loamy soils hold a moderate amount of water and suit most methods.
- Clay soils, including black soils, hold the most water (often 150 to 250 mm per metre) and need less frequent but larger irrigations. Over-irrigation causes waterlogging; furrow or alternate-furrow irrigation and good drainage help. Deep cracks in dry black soil take in water quickly at the first irrigation.

## When to irrigate
Irrigate before the crop shows wilting, ideally when about half of the available soil water in the root zone has been used. A simple check: soil from root depth that crumbles and will not form a ball when squeezed (for loams) usually needs irrigation. Tensiometers or soil moisture sensors give more precise readings. SoilGrids and similar maps do not provide real-time soil moisture.

## Critical growth stages
Water stress at these stages reduces yield the most: rice at panicle initiation and flowering; wheat at crown root initiation, flowering and grain filling; maize at tasselling and silking; pulses at flowering and pod filling; groundnut at pegging and pod development; cotton at flowering and boll formation; sunflower at bud and flowering; vegetables at flowering and fruit development.

## Efficient methods
- Drip irrigation saves about 30 to 50% water compared with flooding and allows fertigation; suitable for vegetables, sugarcane, banana, cotton, orchards and plantations.
- Sprinkler irrigation suits light soils, pulses, groundnut and close-spaced crops.
- Furrow, alternate-furrow and ridge-furrow irrigation save water in row crops on heavier soils.
- Alternate wetting and drying in rice saves water without major yield loss when done correctly.
- Mulching (crop residue or plastic) reduces evaporation and weeds.
- Government schemes in India support micro-irrigation (drip and sprinkler) with subsidies; ask the agriculture or horticulture department.

## Rainfed farming
Where irrigation is not available: conserve rainwater with contour bunds, farm ponds, compartment bunding and deep summer ploughing; choose drought-tolerant crops and short-duration varieties; sow on time with the monsoon; use mulches; and on black soils grow rabi crops on stored soil moisture.
