# Soil improvement and soil health practices

## Build organic matter (all soils)
Add farmyard manure, compost or vermicompost; incorporate crop residues instead of burning them; grow green manures such as sunhemp or dhaincha and plough them in before flowering; grow cover crops; and include legumes in rotation. Organic matter improves soil structure, water holding in sandy soils, drainage and workability in clay soils, nutrient supply and soil life. Raising organic carbon is slow and takes several seasons of consistent practice.

## Improving sandy soils
Add organic matter, use mulch, irrigate lightly and frequently, split fertilizer applications, and use windbreaks where wind erosion occurs. Tank silt or clay application is used in some regions to improve water holding.

## Improving heavy clay and black soils
Provide surface and subsurface drainage, use broad bed and furrow or ridge-furrow systems, till only at the right moisture (not too wet or too dry), add organic matter and avoid compaction from heavy machinery when wet.

## Correcting soil acidity
Apply agricultural lime (calcium carbonate) or dolomite (adds magnesium) based on a lime-requirement test. Lime acts slowly; apply it well before sowing and mix into the soil. Organic matter also buffers acidity.

## Correcting sodic and saline soils
Sodic soils: apply gypsum based on a gypsum-requirement test, add organic matter, ensure drainage and leach with good-quality water; grow tolerant crops during reclamation. Saline soils: leach salts with good-quality water, improve drainage, avoid poor-quality irrigation water, and grow salt-tolerant crops.

## Preventing erosion
Contour farming, contour or graded bunds, terracing on slopes, strip cropping, mulching, cover crops and keeping the soil covered during heavy rain reduce erosion. Red and laterite soils on slopes are especially prone to erosion.

## Reducing compaction
Avoid working wet soil, reduce heavy machinery passes, use deep-rooted crops and occasional subsoiling where a hard pan has formed, and add organic matter.

## Crop rotation and diversification
Rotating cereals with legumes, deep-rooted with shallow-rooted crops, and changing crop families breaks pest and disease cycles and balances nutrient use. Intercropping (for example pigeonpea with sorghum or groundnut) reduces risk in rainfed farming.

## Monitoring
Test soil every 2 to 3 years (the Soil Health Card cycle) to track pH, organic carbon and nutrients, and adjust management accordingly.
