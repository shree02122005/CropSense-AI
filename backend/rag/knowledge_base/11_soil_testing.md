# How to get a soil test

## Why test
A soil test measures the actual nutrients, pH, EC and organic carbon of a field. It is the only reliable basis for fertilizer doses and for diagnosing acidity, salinity or sodicity. Map data such as SoilGrids and app estimates are useful screening tools but cannot replace a test.

## Where to test in India
- Soil Health Card scheme: the state agriculture department collects samples (often through village-level grids) and issues a card with 12 parameters and crop-wise recommendations.
- Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) and state agricultural university soil testing laboratories.
- Government and approved private soil testing laboratories; some offer mobile soil-testing vans.
Ask the local Raitha Samparka Kendra (in Karnataka) or the agriculture office for the nearest facility.

## How to collect a soil sample
1. Collect after harvest and before applying fertilizer or manure.
2. Divide the field into uniform areas; sample each separately if soil, crop or management differs.
3. From each area, take soil from 8 to 10 spots in a zig-zag pattern. Remove surface litter, dig a V-shaped pit to 15 cm (about 0 to 15 cm depth for field crops; deeper for orchards as advised), and take a thin slice from one side.
4. Mix the samples thoroughly in a clean bucket, and reduce to about half a kilogram by quartering.
5. Dry in shade, put in a clean cloth or plastic bag, and label with name, survey number, crop history and date.
6. Avoid sampling near trees, bunds, manure heaps, fertilizer bands or wet spots.

## What to ask the lab for
At minimum: pH, EC, organic carbon, available N, P, K. Also sulphur, zinc, iron, boron and other micronutrients where deficiencies are common, and lime or gypsum requirement for acidic or sodic soils.
